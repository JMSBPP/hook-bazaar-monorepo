
# Preliminaries

- **hooks:** firms producing differentiated services (e.g liquidity management, fee adjustment, etc) (firms of "insumos")
- **pools:** consumers of hook services (firms of final services )
    - Each pool demands specific functionalities
    - Pools choose among competing hooks offering these functions

- Hooks define protocols (firms)
- Hooks with same functionality compete for liquidity and take on protocol fees (firms competing on markets for revenue share)


# Problem

**Pools lack agency over which hooks they use** 

- Pools cannot autonomously or collectively choose which hook(s) to integrate when multiple eligible hooks are available for the same role.

This represents a **missing market mechanism** with several interrelated economic failures:

1. **Two-Sided Market Failure**: There exists supply (competing hooks) (production factor suppliers) and demand (pools needing hook services) (final service firm), but no matching mechanism allows pools to express preferences and select hooks. This prevents efficient market clearing.

- Pools (final service firms) face high costs in deploying their own hooks, with auditing costs, etc.

(This happens when protocols define and develop their own hooks. Such hooks might have vulnerabilities and might not be audited, etc. Having access to a market of curated, audited hooks can lower deployment and operation costs.)
This means from the very start there are high costs and **Allocation Inefficiency**. Without optimal pool choice, hook-pool matching occurs suboptimally. Superior hooks (from pools' perspectives) may not be selected, while inferior hooks are forced upon pools. This represents **Pareto inefficiency** in resource allocation.

5. There is no matching mechanism connecting supply and demand. While hooks compete among themselves, this competition cannot function properly without demand-side choice. This relates to **contestable markets theory**—competition requires that consumers can switch providers.

6. **Coordination Failure**: Even with multiple eligible hooks available, pools cannot coordinate (individually or collectively) to select optimal matches. This prevents the market from achieving equilibrium where supply meets demand at optimal prices and quality levels.

This is only possible if pools, instead of defining a single hook, connect to a market of hooks where they have agency in deciding which functionalities are there.
